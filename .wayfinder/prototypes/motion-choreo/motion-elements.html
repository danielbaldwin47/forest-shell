forest-shell · reference for the motion spec · issue #27

Six surfaces, before we decide how they move.

The vocabulary is already fixed. What is open is which surface takes which step, and what happens between two drawers. This page puts the six surfaces in one place so they can be looked at while that gets decided — real prototype captures where a prototype exists, labelled stand-ins where none does. Nothing here animates and nothing here proposes a timing; the empty tables are the decision, not a draft of it.

Ease
cubic-bezier(0.22, 1, 0.36, 1) — the fog ease. Fog moves, it doesn't snap; nothing bouncy or springy.
Steps
140 · 240 · 320 ms — three steps, and only these three.
Exits
One step faster than the matching entrance.
Scrim
Animates opacity only. Blur never animates.
Budget
8 ms GPU frame time on the T480, zero dropped frames at 60 Hz.

Open

  • Which of 140 / 240 / 320 does the bar reveal take?
  • And the workspace change — where height and opacity change on every slot at once, not just the two that swap.

Open

  • What actually moves when the launcher opens — the scrim's opacity, the card, the rows, or some stagger across them?
  • If the rows stagger, row staggering is the classic way this gets expensive against the 8 ms budget.
  • Inside the list: does the rail and fill travel between rows, or cut? Travel makes the arrow key a movement; cutting makes it a jump.

Layer surface · stacked

Notification toasts

Three separate animations, and the third only exists because the other two do.

mock · in the design language

Step — unassigned
Animation140240320

Open

  • Enter — the brief has new cards drifting in from above and settling. Which step is a drift, and which is a drop?
  • Exit — dismissed cards fade and desaturate into the fog. One step faster than enter, by the rule.
  • Stack-shift — when one card leaves, every card beneath it moves up. Does that share the exit's step, run on its own, or wait until the exit finishes?
  • Whether a burst of three arriving at once staggers or lands together.

Across all six

The questions that aren't per-surface

Reduction tier

What the reducedEffects knob removes — and whether motion needs a reduction tier of its own, separate from the effects one.

Frame budget

8 ms of GPU frame time on the T480 with zero dropped frames at 60 Hz. Which of these animations is at risk, and what the fallback is when one of them is.

The exit rule

Exits run one step faster than entrances. At 140 ms there is no faster step, so anything assigned 140 on the way in has nowhere to go on the way out.
